ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Implementation Guide - Annex A Controls Explained

About this dataset
This guide summarises ISO/IEC 27001:2022, the current version of the standard. Annex A contains 93 controls grouped into four themes: Organizational controls (A.5, 37 controls), People controls (A.6, 8 controls), Physical controls (A.7, 14 controls) and Technological controls (A.8, 34 controls). The descriptions are plain-language explanations written for this project, not the official text of the standard.

Changes from ISO/IEC 27001:2013
The 2013 version had 114 controls in 14 domains (A.5 to A.18). The 2022 version merged them into 93 controls in 4 themes and added 11 new controls: threat intelligence (A.5.7), information security for use of cloud services (A.5.23), ICT readiness for business continuity (A.5.30), physical security monitoring (A.7.4), configuration management (A.8.9), information deletion (A.8.10), data masking (A.8.11), data leakage prevention (A.8.12), monitoring activities (A.8.16), web filtering (A.8.23) and secure coding (A.8.28). Organisations certified to the 2013 version had until October 2025 to transition.

ISMS management clauses 4 to 10
Besides Annex A, the standard has mandatory clauses for the information security management system (ISMS): Clause 4 context of the organization and ISMS scope; Clause 5 leadership and the information security policy; Clause 6 planning, including risk assessment, risk treatment and the Statement of Applicability; Clause 7 support, including resources, competence, awareness and documented information; Clause 8 operation of the risk treatment plan; Clause 9 performance evaluation through monitoring, internal audit and management review; Clause 10 improvement and corrective action.

Risk assessment and Statement of Applicability
ISO 27001 is risk-based. The organisation identifies risks to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information, evaluates their likelihood and impact, and decides how to treat them (modify, avoid, share or accept). The Statement of Applicability (SoA) lists all 93 Annex A controls, says whether each one is applied, and justifies every inclusion and exclusion.

A.5.1 Policies for information security (Organizational controls)
Management must approve an information security policy and topic-specific policies, publish them, communicate them to staff and relevant external parties, and review them at planned intervals or when significant changes happen.

A.5.2 Information security roles and responsibilities (Organizational controls)
Security responsibilities must be defined and assigned according to the organisation's needs, so everyone knows who owns which information security task.

A.5.3 Segregation of duties (Organizational controls)
Conflicting duties and areas of responsibility must be separated, so that no single person can misuse or change assets without authorisation or detection, for example the person who requests a payment should not also approve it.

A.5.4 Management responsibilities (Organizational controls)
Managers must require all personnel to apply information security in line with the organisation's policies and procedures.

A.5.5 Contact with authorities (Organizational controls)
The organisation must establish and maintain contact with relevant authorities such as regulators, law enforcement and data protection authorities.

A.5.6 Contact with special interest groups (Organizational controls)
The organisation should keep contact with security forums, professional associations and special interest groups to stay informed about good practice and emerging threats.

A.5.7 Threat intelligence (Organizational controls) - new in 2022
Information about information security threats must be collected and analysed to produce threat intelligence that informs risk assessments and protective measures.

A.5.8 Information security in project management (Organizational controls)
Information security must be integrated into project management, so security requirements are identified and addressed in every project regardless of type.

A.5.9 Inventory of information and other associated assets (Organizational controls)
An inventory of information and associated assets (hardware, software, services, data), including their owners, must be developed and kept up to date.

A.5.10 Acceptable use of information and other associated assets (Organizational controls)
Rules for the acceptable use and handling of information and assets must be identified, documented and implemented.

A.5.11 Return of assets (Organizational controls)
Personnel and external parties must return all organisational assets in their possession when their employment, contract or agreement changes or ends.

A.5.12 Classification of information (Organizational controls)
Information must be classified according to confidentiality, integrity, availability and legal requirements, for example public, internal, confidential and restricted.

A.5.13 Labelling of information (Organizational controls)
Procedures must exist for labelling information in line with the classification scheme, so people can see how sensitive information is and how to handle it.

A.5.14 Information transfer (Organizational controls)
Rules, procedures or agreements must protect information transferred inside the organisation and to external parties, across all types of transfer such as email, file sharing and physical media.

A.5.15 Access control (Organizational controls)
Rules for physical and logical access to information and assets must be set based on business and security requirements, following the principles of need-to-know and least privilege.

A.5.16 Identity management (Organizational controls)
The full lifecycle of identities (creation, changes and removal of user and system accounts) must be managed, and shared identities should be avoided or tightly controlled.

A.5.17 Authentication information (Organizational controls)
Allocation and management of authentication information such as passwords and tokens must be controlled, including secure delivery and guidance for users on keeping secrets safe.

A.5.18 Access rights (Organizational controls)
Access rights must be provisioned, reviewed regularly, changed and removed according to the access control policy. Regular access reviews catch rights that are no longer needed.

A.5.19 Information security in supplier relationships (Organizational controls)
Processes must manage the information security risks that come from using suppliers' products and services.

A.5.20 Addressing information security within supplier agreements (Organizational controls)
Security requirements must be agreed with each supplier based on the type of relationship, for example confidentiality, data protection, right to audit and incident notification clauses in contracts.

A.5.21 Managing information security in the ICT supply chain (Organizational controls)
Processes must manage security risks in the ICT products and services supply chain, including components and services from sub-suppliers.

A.5.22 Monitoring, review and change management of supplier services (Organizational controls)
The organisation must regularly monitor, review and evaluate supplier security practices and service delivery, and manage changes to them.

A.5.23 Information security for use of cloud services (Organizational controls) - new in 2022
Processes for acquiring, using, managing and exiting cloud services must be defined according to the organisation's security requirements, including shared responsibility with the cloud provider.

A.5.24 Information security incident management planning and preparation (Organizational controls)
The organisation must plan and prepare for incidents by defining incident management processes, roles and responsibilities, in other words it needs an incident response plan.

A.5.25 Assessment and decision on information security events (Organizational controls)
Security events must be assessed to decide whether they should be classified as information security incidents.

A.5.26 Response to information security incidents (Organizational controls)
Incidents must be responded to according to documented procedures, including containment, escalation, communication and recovery.

A.5.27 Learning from information security incidents (Organizational controls)
Knowledge gained from incidents must be used to strengthen controls, for example through lessons-learned reviews after each incident.

A.5.28 Collection of evidence (Organizational controls)
Procedures must exist for identifying, collecting, acquiring and preserving evidence related to security events, so it can be used in disciplinary or legal action.

A.5.29 Information security during disruption (Organizational controls)
The organisation must plan how to keep information security at an appropriate level during disruption such as a disaster or crisis.

A.5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity (Organizational controls) - new in 2022
ICT readiness must be planned, implemented, maintained and tested based on business continuity objectives, for example disaster recovery plans with recovery time and recovery point objectives.

A.5.31 Legal, statutory, regulatory and contractual requirements (Organizational controls)
Legal, regulatory and contractual requirements relevant to information security, and the organisation's approach to meeting them, must be identified, documented and kept up to date.

A.5.32 Intellectual property rights (Organizational controls)
Procedures must protect intellectual property rights, including correct licensing of software.

A.5.33 Protection of records (Organizational controls)
Records must be protected from loss, destruction, falsification, unauthorised access and unauthorised release.

A.5.34 Privacy and protection of PII (Organizational controls)
The organisation must identify and meet requirements for preserving privacy and protecting personally identifiable information (PII) under applicable laws such as GDPR.

A.5.35 Independent review of information security (Organizational controls)
The organisation's approach to information security must be reviewed independently at planned intervals or when significant changes occur, for example by internal audit or an external party.

A.5.36 Compliance with policies, rules and standards for information security (Organizational controls)
Compliance with the security policy, topic-specific policies, rules and standards must be reviewed regularly.

A.5.37 Documented operating procedures (Organizational controls)
Operating procedures for information processing facilities must be documented and made available to the personnel who need them.

A.6.1 Screening (People controls)
Background verification checks on candidates must be carried out before they join, proportionate to business requirements, the classification of information they will access and the risks, and in line with laws.

A.6.2 Terms and conditions of employment (People controls)
Employment contracts must state the personnel's and the organisation's responsibilities for information security.

A.6.3 Information security awareness, education and training (People controls)
Personnel must receive appropriate security awareness, education and training, plus regular updates on relevant policies and procedures. Phishing awareness is a common example.

A.6.4 Disciplinary process (People controls)
A formal and communicated disciplinary process must exist for personnel who violate the information security policy.

A.6.5 Responsibilities after termination or change of employment (People controls)
Security responsibilities that remain valid after someone leaves or changes role must be defined and communicated. Access rights must be removed promptly when employment ends.

A.6.6 Confidentiality or non-disclosure agreements (People controls)
Confidentiality or non-disclosure agreements reflecting the organisation's needs must be identified, documented, reviewed regularly and signed by personnel and relevant external parties.

A.6.7 Remote working (People controls)
Security measures must be in place when personnel work remotely, to protect information accessed, processed or stored outside the organisation's premises, for example VPN use, device encryption and rules for working in public places.

A.6.8 Information security event reporting (People controls)
The organisation must provide a way for personnel to report observed or suspected security events quickly through appropriate channels.

A.7.1 Physical security perimeters (Physical controls)
Security perimeters must be defined and used to protect areas that contain information and other associated assets.

A.7.2 Physical entry (Physical controls)
Secure areas must be protected by appropriate entry controls and access points, such as badges, reception desks and visitor logs.

A.7.3 Securing offices, rooms and facilities (Physical controls)
Physical security for offices, rooms and facilities must be designed and implemented, for example locked server rooms.

A.7.4 Physical security monitoring (Physical controls) - new in 2022
Premises must be continuously monitored for unauthorised physical access, for example with CCTV, alarms and intrusion detection.

A.7.5 Protecting against physical and environmental threats (Physical controls)
Protection against physical and environmental threats such as fire, flood, earthquake, power surges and other natural or man-made threats must be designed and implemented.

A.7.6 Working in secure areas (Physical controls)
Security measures for working in secure areas must be designed and implemented, for example restricting photography and unsupervised work.

A.7.7 Clear desk and clear screen (Physical controls)
Clear desk rules for papers and removable storage media, and clear screen rules for information processing facilities, must be defined and enforced. In practice staff lock their screens when they step away and do not leave sensitive documents on desks.

A.7.8 Equipment siting and protection (Physical controls)
Equipment must be sited securely and protected from environmental threats and unauthorised access.

A.7.9 Security of assets off-premises (Physical controls)
Assets used outside the organisation's premises, such as laptops and phones, must be protected.

A.7.10 Storage media (Physical controls)
Storage media such as USB drives and external disks must be managed through their whole lifecycle of acquisition, use, transport and disposal, according to the classification scheme.

A.7.11 Supporting utilities (Physical controls)
Information processing facilities must be protected from power failures and other disruptions caused by failures in supporting utilities, for example with UPS and backup generators.

A.7.12 Cabling security (Physical controls)
Cables carrying power, data or supporting services must be protected from interception, interference or damage.

A.7.13 Equipment maintenance (Physical controls)
Equipment must be maintained correctly to ensure the availability, integrity and confidentiality of information.

A.7.14 Secure disposal or re-use of equipment (Physical controls)
Equipment containing storage media must be checked so that sensitive data and licensed software are removed or securely overwritten before disposal or re-use.

A.8.1 User endpoint devices (Technological controls)
Information stored on, processed by or accessible through user endpoint devices such as laptops and mobile phones must be protected, for example with device encryption, screen locks and mobile device management.

A.8.2 Privileged access rights (Technological controls)
The allocation and use of privileged access rights such as administrator accounts must be restricted and managed.

A.8.3 Information access restriction (Technological controls)
Access to information and other associated assets must be restricted according to the access control policy.

A.8.4 Access to source code (Technological controls)
Read and write access to source code, development tools and software libraries must be appropriately managed.

A.8.5 Secure authentication (Technological controls)
Secure authentication technologies and procedures must be implemented based on access restrictions and the access control policy. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is the usual measure for critical systems and remote access.

A.8.6 Capacity management (Technological controls)
The use of resources must be monitored and adjusted in line with current and expected capacity requirements.

A.8.7 Protection against malware (Technological controls)
Protection against malware must be implemented and supported by user awareness, for example anti-malware or EDR tools on endpoints and servers.

A.8.8 Management of technical vulnerabilities (Technological controls)
Information about technical vulnerabilities must be obtained, the organisation's exposure evaluated and appropriate measures taken, such as timely patching and vulnerability scanning.

A.8.9 Configuration management (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Configurations, including security configurations, of hardware, software, services and networks must be established, documented, implemented, monitored and reviewed, for example secure baselines and hardening standards.

A.8.10 Information deletion (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Information stored in systems, devices or other storage media must be deleted when it is no longer required, in line with retention rules and privacy requirements.

A.8.11 Data masking (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Data masking such as pseudonymisation or anonymisation must be used according to the access control policy and business and legal requirements, especially for personal data.

A.8.12 Data leakage prevention (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Data leakage prevention (DLP) measures must be applied to systems, networks and devices that process, store or transmit sensitive information.

A.8.13 Information backup (Technological controls)
Backup copies of information, software and systems must be maintained and regularly tested according to the agreed backup policy.

A.8.14 Redundancy of information processing facilities (Technological controls)
Information processing facilities must have enough redundancy to meet availability requirements.

A.8.15 Logging (Technological controls)
Logs recording activities, exceptions, faults and other relevant events must be produced, stored, protected and analysed.

A.8.16 Monitoring activities (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Networks, systems and applications must be monitored for anomalous behaviour, and appropriate action taken to evaluate potential incidents, for example through a SIEM or security operations centre.

A.8.17 Clock synchronization (Technological controls)
Clocks of information processing systems must be synchronised to approved time sources so that logs can be correlated.

A.8.18 Use of privileged utility programs (Technological controls)
The use of utility programs that can override system and application controls must be restricted and tightly controlled.

A.8.19 Installation of software on operational systems (Technological controls)
Procedures and measures must securely manage software installation on operational systems.

A.8.20 Networks security (Technological controls)
Networks and network devices must be secured, managed and controlled to protect information in systems and applications.

A.8.21 Security of network services (Technological controls)
Security mechanisms, service levels and service requirements of network services must be identified, implemented and monitored.

A.8.22 Segregation of networks (Technological controls)
Groups of information services, users and systems must be segregated in the organisation's networks, for example separate network segments for guests, users and servers based on risk.

A.8.23 Web filtering (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Access to external websites must be managed to reduce exposure to malicious content.

A.8.24 Use of cryptography (Technological controls)
Rules for the effective use of cryptography, including cryptographic key management, must be defined and implemented. Data should be encrypted in transit and at rest using industry-standard algorithms.

A.8.25 Secure development life cycle (Technological controls)
Rules for the secure development of software and systems must be established and applied throughout the software development life cycle (SDLC).

A.8.26 Application security requirements (Technological controls)
Information security requirements must be identified, specified and approved when developing or acquiring applications.

A.8.27 Secure system architecture and engineering principles (Technological controls)
Principles for engineering secure systems must be established, documented, maintained and applied to all development activities, such as defence in depth and secure by design.

A.8.28 Secure coding (Technological controls) - new in 2022
Secure coding principles must be applied to software development, for example input validation, avoiding hard-coded secrets and following OWASP guidance.

A.8.29 Security testing in development and acceptance (Technological controls)
Security testing processes must be defined and implemented in the development life cycle, such as code reviews, static analysis and penetration testing before release.

A.8.30 Outsourced development (Technological controls)
The organisation must direct, monitor and review activities related to outsourced system development.

A.8.31 Separation of development, test and production environments (Technological controls)
Development, testing and production environments must be separated and secured.

A.8.32 Change management (Technological controls)
Changes to information processing facilities and systems must follow change management procedures, including approval, testing and rollback planning.

A.8.33 Test information (Technological controls)
Test information must be appropriately selected, protected and managed, for example production personal data should not be copied into test environments without masking.

A.8.34 Protection of information systems during audit testing (Technological controls)
Audit tests and other assurance activities involving operational systems must be planned and agreed between the tester and management to minimise impact on business operations.

Scope of this guide
This guide does not include pricing of ISO certification, names of consultants or certification bodies, specific audit checklists, vendor or tool recommendations, or the official wording of the standard. For the authoritative text, refer to the published ISO/IEC 27001:2022 standard.
